AI/LLM generated code policy

TL;DR:

    AIs/LLMs are useful tools when used surgically to assist a human. As long
    as they are used to assist, not replace human development their use is
    acceptable. Generated code requires strict human review, and must be
    clearly noted as being AI/LLM generated. AI/LLM generated code must also be
    in small chunks that are easy for a human to digest.

    No 'vibe' coded contributions where no human understands what was produced
    or how it works will be accepted. Such things are fine for rapid
    experimentation, just not final product.

    Bot and AI accounts do not open pull requests or take part in ticket
    discussion. The one exception is filing an initial bug report, which must
    disclose that it comes from a bot and then leave the thread to humans.
    Humans may submit AI-assisted work; the human who submits it does the
    talking.

    These rules exist to serve two goals. First, the humans who maintain this
    code later must be able to understand it. Second, the burden that
    externally submitted AI/LLM generated or assisted work places on the core
    maintainers has to stay manageable. They are not a standard the repository
    is held to. Proven contributors have some leeway where both goals are
    still met.

Who these rules are for, and how strict they are

    This policy manages a burden. It does not certify a standard.

    Every outside contribution is a request for the core maintainers' time and
    attention. Someone has to read it, understand it, judge whether it is
    right, and then own it for years after the contributor has moved on. A
    one-time contributor cannot carry any of that, and cannot be asked to.
    These rules exist so that what arrives is something the people who stay
    can actually take on.

    The bar every contribution must clear is that the humans maintaining the
    code going forward can understand it. The rest of this policy is a guide
    for getting new contributors and new contributions over that bar. It is
    not an unbreakable law.

    The core maintainers are not bound by it. In practice they mostly work
    this way anyway, because they are the ones who would pay for not doing so.
    But a maintainer who does something differently is not being a hypocrite:
    this is not a repository-wide standard being enforced, and the policy has
    nothing to say about the work of the people the burden already falls on.
    Their accountability is the maintenance itself, which they do not get to
    hand off.

    Active project members with a track record of quality work have more
    freedom. They may deviate from the specifics below — how work is chunked,
    whether corrections land as their own commit, how AI use is noted — as
    long as what they contribute stays high quality and understandable to the
    people who will maintain it.

    How much flexibility applies depends on the contribution itself and on
    proven contributors being willing to do the review. A larger or looser
    contribution is acceptable when someone trusted has actually read it and
    stands behind it. When that is not the case, follow the rules as written.

Can developers use AI/LLM tools to contribute?

    Developers who contribute are allowed to use whatever tools they decide are
    necessary for their work. The project will not micromanage a contributor's
    workflow. This includes AI/LLM tools.

Tickets and pull requests are for humans

    Pull requests and tickets are where humans discuss the project with each
    other. Do not use a bot, AI, or LLM account to open a pull request, review
    one, comment on one, or interact with a ticket. A maintainer who wants an
    agent's take can run their own agent against the discussion; they do not
    need one posting in the thread.

    This is about who is in the conversation, not about what wrote the code. A
    human may absolutely open a pull request containing AI/LLM-assisted work —
    that is what the rest of this policy governs, and nothing here restricts
    it. What the human cannot do is hand the conversation off. If you submit
    it, you answer for it: you respond to review, you explain the decisions,
    and you make the corrections. The same assist-versus-replace line applies
    here as everywhere else in this policy — use whatever tools help you write
    a reply, but the reply has to be one you understand and stand behind, not
    one you relayed.

    There is one exception: an initial bug report may be filed by a bot, AI, or
    LLM account. That is welcome, and especially so for security issues. When
    filing one:

        - State plainly, in the report itself, that this is a bot account and
          that an AI/LLM wrote the report.

        - Give the full context needed to understand and debug the issue —
          affected versions, environment, reproduction steps, and the observed
          and expected behavior. A report a human cannot act on without asking
          the bot follow-up questions defeats the exception.

        - Stop there. Do not comment further, answer questions, argue, or
          submit a fix. Humans take it from that point, and any maintainer who
          wants agent help will use their own.

AI/LLM code MUST be human vetted

    Any code that goes in should be vetted by a human. Maintainers who merge
    pull requests MUST verify the accuracy and utility of any PR.

    If the AI/LLM generated code needs additional edits or corrections
    before/during merge, those MUST be a separate commit indicating what human
    actions were needed to correct the generated code.

AI/LLM code MUST be in digestible chunks

    This applies to any code, but LLMs have a greater capacity for generating
    large PRs that are too big for a human to digest and verify.

    Overly large PRs must be broken up into smaller commits, and when possible
    smaller PRs. If humans cannot reasonably verify a PR it should not be
    accepted.

No 'vibe' coding

    A human must understand what is being done from start to finish. AI may
    assist, and be used as a tool. But it cannot do all the work, and it cannot
    replace your own understanding of the code you are contributing.

Documentation follows the project's documentation policy

    Documentation may be AI written, and may even be identifiably so, but it
    must meet the same readability and content standards as human-written
    documentation. It must be concise, natural to read, useful to its audience,
    and free of needless repetition.

Significant AI/LLM use MUST be noted

    Say so in the commit message, the pull request description, or the ticket.
    A plain sentence is enough.

    The reason is mundane. The maintainers want a rough sense of how much of
    what comes in is AI/LLM generated or assisted, and how much is purely
    human. That is tracked loosely and mentally, from what the pull requests
    and tickets happen to say over time. It is not counted, not audited, not
    recorded anywhere in the code, and no number it produces triggers
    anything. A secondary benefit: it tells us later whether there is a human
    who understood the change well enough to be asked about it.

    What to note is work written primarily by or with an LLM, as opposed to
    work a human wrote with a little AI help or input. We are not concerned
    with the middle ground, only with cases where there is significant AI/LLM
    content.

    You do not need to note:

        - Using AI for code review.

        - Using AI to correct a minor syntax issue.

        - Using AI to translate or write small code snippets.

    A good rule to go by: if it is something small enough that you could
    reasonably ask a peer to help with on short notice, or find quickly with a
    search, it probably does not need to be noted as AI.

    If code reads as AI-written but is presented as written by a human, you
    will be asked to correct that.

    This is not an exhaustive audit and not a way to single out contributors
    who use or reject AI assistance. Using this rule as an excuse to troll or
    attack a contributor will not be tolerated. If you think someone missed
    the mark on noting AI use, kindly ask them to clarify and potentially
    update their PR.
